# Okwethu five minute submission video script

This script is for an edited five-minute screen recording for the OpenAI and Andela African storytelling hackathon. The primary track is Truth, Authorship and Ownership. Narrate only the quoted paragraphs; the screen actions and captions are production cues. The timeline totals **300 seconds**, including opening and closing cards.

The draft contains **536 spoken words**. Rehearse at approximately 125–135 words per minute, with short pauses for screen actions. The timestamps are an editing plan, not a guarantee of a particular speaker's delivery time. Record in sections, cut typing and page-loading waits, and trim the final edit to 5:00.

## Prepare the walkthrough

1. Confirm the site you will record has the current community-tools release and passes the relevant checks in [the testing guide](TESTING.md). Use a clearly labelled fictional example; keep Demo labels and honest-limits copy visible. Use your actual Okwethu logo/screens rather than invented cultural imagery. Any additional photographs or recordings should be your own or used with permission and appropriate credit. The forwarded-story opening can be a simple illustration made from the fictional demo card.
2. Prepare one story with three contributors: fictional storyteller **40%**, fictional community fund with representative **40%**, fictional translator **20%**. Grant PUBLISH for all; leave the storyteller's COMMERCIAL consent initially refused and AI consent/terms off. Choose education free with credit/commercial use by permission.
3. Use a fictional booklet request and a **ZAR 100** demonstration fee. Request/final-fee inputs use minor units: enter **10000**, not 100. The registration calculator uses ordinary ZAR: enter **100**. The final ledger rows should be ZAR 40, ZAR 40 and ZAR 20 for that request.
4. Record the wizard sections in sequence, then use the resulting passport for the request, blocked approval, recorded consent change and final allocation. Keep the story ID and terms consistent. Leave time between recordings to prepare the next form; edit out typing rather than pretending an unperformed action succeeded.
5. Sign in before recording. Keep a separate visitor window and prepared steward tabs for Manage story, Permission inbox and the story-filtered ledger. Use clean placeholder contact details except when a genuine mailbox is required. Do not show passwords, private settings, unrelated requests or full private-link tokens in the published recording. A close crop can show the tracking message/status while keeping the address bar out of frame.
6. The main walkthrough demonstrates direct approval after recorded consent. Counteroffers, online counteroffer responses, search, roles and history can be shown as short cutaways using separate clearly labelled fictional records; do not imply the main request took a different path. If showing a counteroffer, use the actual workflow and preserve its final-fee agreement.
7. Prepare a QR-marked image download and a PDF page for cutaways. Do not wait for a complete real-time video export during the five-minute recording. Show optional email as configured capability; show inbox delivery only if you have actually tested it with a controlled recipient.
8. Use a legible screen capture, natural pointer movements and slow scrolling. Keep captions short and use the app's goldenrod `#DAA520`, cream and ink colours. Read the script aloud once before recording and check the current site's behavior against [the testing guide](TESTING.md).

## Timed script and screen actions

### 0:00–0:25 — The problem

**Show:** Start with the Okwethu logo, then a simple animation of a fictional story card being forwarded between three phones. The creator credit fades as it moves. At 0:12, dissolve into the actual homepage. Use a small “Illustrative example” caption for the animation.

**Say:**

> A story can travel across the world in seconds. As it is copied and forwarded, the people behind it can disappear: their names, their context, and their wishes. For African storytellers and communities, reaching an audience should also mean keeping a say in how their work is used.

### 0:25–0:50 — Introduce Okwethu and the track

**Show:** Show the homepage and logo, with a small presenter camera inset if desired. Add the lower-third “Truth, Authorship & Ownership”. Briefly overlay “Built with AI-assisted development using Codex”; do not show prompts or code.

**Say:**

> Okwethu means “Ours” in isiZulu. My project is a Story Passport and Community Ledger, built for the Truth, Authorship and Ownership track. I used AI-assisted development with Codex to build this working prototype. It gives people a place to record credit, consent, terms and agreed benefit-sharing.

### 0:50–1:20 — Register people and consent

**Show:** In Steward desk → Register a story, show the prepared story details and then the People step. Use a fictional record labelled “Demo placeholder story”. Point to language/context, then RECORD, PUBLISH, TRANSLATE, COMMERCIAL and AI_TRAINING. Leave the storyteller’s COMMERCIAL choice unchecked; everyone grants PUBLISH.

**Say:**

> I’ll use a clearly labelled fictional example. A creator or community representative registers text, an image, audio or video, with its language and context. Each contributor is named, and consent is recorded separately for recording, publishing, translation, commercial use and AI training. Agreeing to publish is never treated as agreement to every other use.

### 1:20–1:45 — Declare terms and benefit-sharing

**Show:** Show Terms: education free, commercial use by permission, AI training off. Move to Shares with three prepared contributors at 40%, 40% and 20%. Set the calculator sample amount to 100 ZAR and briefly show 40 / 40 / 20. Show explicit confirmations, review and register; cut typing/loading.

**Say:**

> The people involved choose their declared terms and shares. Here, education is free with credit, commercial use needs permission, and AI training is off. Forty percent goes to the storyteller, forty to a community fund, and twenty to a translator. The calculator previews the agreement.

### 1:45–2:15 — A passport that travels

**Show:** Open the resulting passport and scroll slowly through creator, consent and terms. Frame the QR, Copy attribution and Download passport PDF buttons. Insert a brief capture of an actual downloaded QR-marked image and a clean PDF page. Keep the main passport code consistent through the demonstration.

**Say:**

> The story now has a public passport: credited people, declared consent, terms and shares in one place. A QR code links back to the current record. Visitors can copy the preferred credit or download a passport PDF. QR-marked image and video exports, and audio-cover packages, help attribution travel with the material. Marks can still be removed.

### 2:15–2:50 — Demonstrate a blocked request

**Show:** Switch to a separate visitor window. Submit a prepared commercial request for a fictional booklet: proposedFeeMinor 10000, currency ZAR, AI unchecked. Show the private tracking link without lingering on the token. Switch to the steward inbox, attempt APPROVED with finalFeeMinor 10000 and fee confirmation, and hold on the missing-commercial-consent error.

**Say:**

> A visitor can describe a proposed use, request permission and suggest a fee. They receive a private tracking link; optional email updates are available when configured. The steward can approve, decline or counteroffer. Here I’m requesting a commercial use. Approval is blocked because one contributor has not agreed. The system keeps that missing consent visible and prevents the approval.

### 2:50–3:25 — Record consent and show the ledger

**Show:** Return to Manage story. For this fictional example, record the storyteller’s newly declared COMMERCIAL consent and a reason. Save; return to the same request, confirm a final fee of 10000 minor units (ZAR 100), approve, then open its story-filtered ledger. Hold on the 40 / 40 / 20 allocations and Demo notice.

**Say:**

> In this fictional example, the storyteller now agrees to that use. The steward records the change with a reason, then confirms the final fee and approves. For a demonstration fee of one hundred rand, the ledger allocates forty rand, forty rand and twenty rand. These are simulated allocations. No real money moves, and earlier decisions remain recorded.

### 3:25–4:00 — Ongoing choice and accountability

**Show:** Use short recorded cutaways: Manage story → Sharing and visibility, a paused version of this passport in the visitor window, Private version history, Community team roles and the conflicting-claim form. Prepare these cuts in advance; do not type through every setting during this beat.

**Say:**

> Consent is ongoing. Withdrawing publication consent or pausing sharing hides public content. Copies already downloaded cannot be pulled back. Private version history retains changes, while community roles control who can maintain records and who can only review them. Visitors can also report a conflicting claim. These features support an ongoing conversation about whose story it is and who may speak for it.

### 4:00–4:30 — Explain the impact and fit to the brief

**Show:** Return to the homepage or an open passport on a phone-sized view. Overlay three short captions: “Credit that stays connected”, “Consent that stays specific”, “Benefits agreed up front”. Briefly show the search controls, then a simple arrow diagram linking creator, passport, requester and community fund.

**Say:**

> This responds to the brief by putting African storytellers’ choices inside the sharing process. A teacher can find whom to ask. A publisher can see the declared terms. Contributors can see how benefits would be shared. It could support community archives, education and community-led tourism, while keeping the people and context connected to the story.

### 4:30–5:00 — Limits, next steps and close

**Show:** Return to the presenter or homepage, then finish on a goldenrod/cream end card with the Okwethu logo and okwethu.wilmans.co.za. Keep “Working proof of concept · Demo allocations” visible. End exactly at 5:00, including any logo hold or fade.

**Say:**

> This is a working proof of concept. It records declared consent and terms; it does not verify ownership or enforce rights. Payments are simulated. The next step is testing with community partners and developing governance and payment partnerships. Okwethu means ours: our stories, our choices, and a clearer record of how we share them.

## Check the submission claims

- The narration credits AI-assisted software development with Codex. It does not claim the app translates stories, uses an in-app generative AI model, automatically verifies sources/ownership, detects plagiarism or makes real payouts.
- The primary brief fit is declared authorship, purpose-specific consent, community terms, requests and visible benefit-sharing. Search/QR support finding the record; no unbuilt cross-language or recommendation capability is claimed.
- An account, timestamp, hash, review badge or QR does not verify cultural authority or ownership. Terms are declared records, and conflicting claims remain possible.
- Restriction/withdrawal protects future responses from the site; it cannot recall earlier copies. Image/video marks can be removed and an audio cover must stay with its file.
- Community archives, education, community-led tourism and future revenue are potential uses, not measured outcomes or evidence of a completed community pilot.
- The main fee is a simulation. An approval changes the demo ledger, not a bank balance. Payment partners and community governance are next steps.

## Fallback and final edit

If recording a click fails, pause, fix it and re-record that section. Keep the actual consent block and a real successful decision/ledger in the finished video. A clean capture from the same working demo is an acceptable cutaway; do not label an unperformed action as successful.

If hosting is unavailable, record the local application and clearly identify it as a local demonstration. A local QR that a phone cannot reach is not a successful hosted-phone scan. If email is not configured, keep the narration about optional email conditional and demonstrate the private tracking page instead.

Before submitting, check that the audio is clear, the site text is readable, every quoted line matches a working feature, private credentials/tokens are concealed, and the final video is exactly 5:00 including any end fade. Keep the original [three-minute presenter script](DEMO.md) separate; this five-minute script is tailored to the submission request.
